[02 | 02_Why_Timing_Matters_en.jpg]
When we think about healthy ageing, it is not just a question of what we take. We also need to ask: when?

[03 | 03_Daily_Supplement_en.jpg]
Imagine you start paying more attention to nutrition. You choose a supplement, and remember to take it every day.

[04 | 04_Delayed_Lunch_en.jpg]
But when life gets busy, mealtimes keep changing. Lunch gets pushed back again.

[05 | 05_Late_Night_Phone_en.jpg]
At night, a little more time on your phone turns into another late bedtime.

[06 | 06_Inside_the_Cell_en.jpg]
So, is taking a supplement enough? To explore that question, let us look inside the cell: a city that works around the clock.

[07 | 07_Mitochondria_Energy_en.jpg]
Mitochondria are like power stations. They help supply the energy cells need to function.

[08 | 08_Daily_Movement_en.jpg]
Every walk, every movement, depends on energy supplied by your cells.

[09 | 09_Lysosome_Recycling_en.jpg]
This city also has recycling centres. Lysosomes break down and recycle some damaged proteins and cellular components.

[10 | 10_DNA_Repair_en.jpg]
There is maintenance work, too. DNA repair, for example, helps cells maintain the integrity of their genetic material.

[11 | 11_Cellular_Ageing_en.jpg]
As we age, some energy-producing, clearing and maintenance functions may become less effective. Damage can gradually accumulate, potentially affecting cells, tissues and organs.

[12 | 12_Morning_Light_en.jpg]
But cellular activity also follows a timetable. Morning light is an important signal that helps set that timing.

[13 | 13_Circadian_Coordination_en.jpg]
Light influences the master clock in the brain, which helps coordinate clocks in different organs and cells. Metabolism and some maintenance processes are regulated by circadian rhythms. Ageing, cellular function and these rhythms can also influence one another.

[14 | 14_Winding_Down_en.jpg]
That is why sleep and daily rhythms deserve attention together. But repair does not happen only at night. Different processes follow different rhythms.

[15 | 15_Meal_Timing_Metabolism_en.jpg]
Mealtimes can also influence some peripheral clocks and metabolic rhythms. What we eat, and when we eat, are two questions worth considering together.

[16 | 16_Ingredient_Research_en.jpg]
Studying an ingredient is a little like studying how to improve one power station. To look after the whole city, we also need to consider the bigger picture.

[17 | 17_Whole_Body_Systems_en.jpg]
Nutrition and metabolism provide materials and energy. Hormones carry regulatory signals, while the immune system also helps shape the cellular environment. Cellular function and whole-body systems continually influence one another.

[18 | 18_Research_to_Practice_en.jpg]
This is the ASCLEPIOS HEALTH approach: a whole-system perspective, respect for biological rhythms, and practical application. Start with the foundations of diet, movement and sleep. Then consider nutritional support according to individual needs and evidence. What to use, how to use it, and when to use it must be considered individually. Not every supplement belongs in a fixed morning, afternoon or evening slot.
