## 2. Customer-problem framing

### Customer may recognise
- Wants a simple evening supplement within an established wind-down routine.
- Is already considering magnesium and wants clear, non-hyped guidance.
- Wants physical-product support integrated with sleep timing, behaviour and education rather than relying on a pill alone.

### Do NOT automatically infer
- The customer is magnesium deficient.
- Magnesium is the cause or cure for their sleep problem.
- More magnesium means better sleep.
- A supplement should replace assessment for persistent insomnia, sleep apnoea, restless legs, medication effects, anxiety/depression or other underlying causes.

### Product role
**A bounded nutritional support option inside a wider sleep-management system — not “a sleeping pill in supplement form.”**

## 3. Suitability and pre-use information

Final suitability wording depends on formula and label, but implementation must reserve visible fields for:
- age restriction / adult-use policy;
- pregnancy/breastfeeding advice;
- relevant kidney/renal disease warning;
- medication interaction/signposting wording where applicable;
- allergy/excipient warnings;
- total daily dose and duplicate-magnesium intake warning;
- gastrointestinal tolerance guidance where applicable to the final magnesium form;
- product-specific contraindications created by any non-magnesium ingredients.

### Higher-caution / CS escalation signals
Do not give personalised dosing advice where the customer reports:
- significant kidney disease or impaired renal function;
- pregnancy/breastfeeding without appropriate professional advice;
- complex medication use or known interaction concern;
- significant unexplained symptoms or suspected sleep disorder;
- adverse reaction after use;
- intent to take materially above label dose.

CS rule: **Never diagnose magnesium deficiency from sleep symptoms alone.**

## 4. Stop-use / adverse-effect route

Final wording must follow label/regulatory review. Minimum operating rule:
- Stop use and seek appropriate advice if a significant allergic reaction or concerning symptoms occur.
- Do not encourage customers to exceed the labelled dose because the expected effect was not felt.
- Record SKU, lot/batch, dose used, timing, other products taken and symptom details for product-quality/adverse-event review where appropriate.
- Repeated diarrhoea or significant GI intolerance should trigger product-use review rather than “push through” advice.

## 5. Claims policy

Because the exact formulation is not yet locked, claims are separated into **structural placeholders** rather than commercial promises.

### CLAIM GREEN — potentially usable once final formula/label substantiates them
- Factual magnesium form(s), elemental amount, serving count and ingredient facts.
- Factual directions for use and storage.
- “Food supplement” / relevant lawful category wording.
- Approved nutrition/health claim wording only where the final product satisfies all conditions of use for the target market.
- “Part of an evening routine” as lifestyle positioning, provided it is not written as a guaranteed sleep outcome.

### CLAIM AMBER — evidence + regulatory wording required before publication
- Helps relaxation.
- Supports sleep quality.
- Helps fall asleep faster.
- Reduces night waking.
- Supports stress resilience or calm.
- Better absorbed / gentler on stomach / superior bioavailability.
- “Best form of magnesium for sleep.”

These are formulation- and evidence-sensitive and must not be generated from generic magnesium literature alone.

### CLAIM RED — prohibited in launch copy unless the regulatory/evidence position materially changes
- Treats/cures insomnia.
- Works like a sleeping pill.
- Treats anxiety/depression.
- Treats restless legs syndrome.
- Corrects a magnesium deficiency without assessment/evidence.
- Guaranteed deeper/REM sleep.
- Guaranteed effect in a fixed number of minutes/nights.
- “Clinically proven to cure sleep problems.”
- “Safe for everyone.”

Marketing rule: **Do not convert a permitted nutrient/function claim into a disease-treatment or guaranteed sleep claim.**

## 7. FAQ master — safe draft

### Is this a sleeping pill?
No. It is a food-supplement product intended to sit within a broader evening routine. It should not be presented as a medicine or as a treatment for insomnia.

### Which type of magnesium is it?
The final commercial page must state the exact magnesium form(s). We should not claim glycinate, citrate, oxide or another form until the production formula is confirmed.

### How much elemental magnesium is in each serving?
PENDING final formula and label. The website must show the elemental amount, not only the weight of the magnesium compound.

### Will it make me sleep better tonight?
Do not promise an immediate or guaranteed sleep effect. Sleep is influenced by timing, stress/arousal, environment, habits, health conditions and other factors; the product is one optional support within that system.

### Can I take more if I do not feel anything?
No. Follow the final labelled dose. CS must not advise exceeding it.

### Can I take it with medication?
Because interactions depend on the medicine and final formula, customers with medication concerns should follow the label and seek pharmacist/health-professional advice rather than receiving personalised interaction advice from generic CS.

### What if it upsets my stomach?
Stop or review use if significant gastrointestinal intolerance occurs and follow the final product guidance. The exact tolerability wording depends on the magnesium form and dose.

## 9. Sleep Intelligence bridge

Magnesium should not be surfaced simply because a user reports “poor sleep.”

### Candidate contextual signals
- User is looking for an optional evening nutritional-support habit.
- No safety/caution signal requiring professional advice.
- Product is compatible with the user’s declared current supplement/medication context within the limits of approved rules.
- Behavioural sleep fundamentals still remain visible; product recommendation must not suppress higher-value routine actions.

### Do-not-recommend / suppress signals
- User wants treatment for suspected sleep disorder.
- Significant renal disease/contraindication signal.
- Unresolved medication interaction concern.
- Prior significant adverse reaction.
- User intends to exceed label dose.
- Final formula data is incomplete.

### Outcome tracking
Track bounded consumer outcomes, not diagnostic conclusions:
- used / not used;
- dose according to label: yes/no;
- timing;
- GI tolerance;
- perceived ease of routine adherence;
- perceived relaxation / sleep experience only as subjective self-report;
- discontinued and reason.

Do not infer magnesium deficiency or clinical treatment response from these signals.
