## 0. Purpose
This object is the single reusable product truth for the Asclepios Sleep Tape launch SKU. Website copy, FAQ, customer-service answers, app guidance, education and marketing should derive from this object rather than creating independent claims.

Core rule: **Sleep Tape is a behavioural/support product, not a treatment for snoring, obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), sleep-disordered breathing or nasal obstruction.** Existing evidence for mouth taping is limited and mixed; indiscriminate use can create risk, particularly where nasal obstruction is present.

## 2. Customer problem — safe framing

### Customer may recognise
- Waking with the mouth open.
- Dry-mouth sensation on waking.
- A habit of sleeping with lips apart despite being able to breathe freely through the nose.
- Wanting a simple physical reminder within a bedtime routine.

### Do NOT automatically infer
- That the customer has OSA.
- That mouth breathing is the cause of their sleep problem.
- That closing the lips will improve oxygen, cure snoring, improve deep sleep or fix an anatomical airway problem.

### Product role
**A small behavioural cue, not an airway treatment.**

Preferred customer-facing bridge:
> “For suitable adults who can breathe comfortably through the nose, Sleep Tape provides a gentle cue to help keep the lips together as part of a calm bedtime routine.”

---

## 3. Who it is for

Potentially suitable adult user:
- Can breathe comfortably through the nose while awake and lying down.
- Has no current nasal blockage/congestion.
- Is not using the product to self-treat suspected sleep apnoea or another breathing disorder.
- Understands that the product is optional and should be removed if uncomfortable.
- Can apply/remove it independently and respond normally if breathing feels restricted.

### Not-for-everyone rule
Product discovery and checkout content must make suitability visible **before** purchase, not hide it inside a long FAQ.

---

## 4. Do-not-use / stop-use safety screen

### Do not use when
- Nose is blocked or breathing through the nose is not comfortable.
- Current heavy nasal congestion, acute sinus infection or similar obstruction is present.
- A clinician has advised against mouth taping/oral occlusion.
- User intends to substitute it for prescribed CPAP, oral appliance or other OSA treatment.
- User cannot reliably remove the tape independently or communicate distress.

### Medical review / extra caution before use
Escalate rather than market around uncertainty where the person reports:
- Diagnosed or suspected OSA / sleep-disordered breathing.
- Loud persistent snoring with witnessed pauses, choking/gasping, or marked daytime sleepiness.
- Structural nasal obstruction, marked deviated septum, enlarged tonsils or chronic severe allergy/congestion.
- Significant respiratory or cardiac disease.
- Recurrent panic/claustrophobia triggered by covering the mouth.
- Significant skin/adhesive allergy history around the lips.

### Stop use immediately if
- Breathing feels restricted.
- User wakes gasping/choking or feels air hunger.
- Significant anxiety/panic occurs.
- Skin develops significant irritation, swelling or allergic reaction.
- Product causes repeated awakenings or worsens sleep.

CS safety rule: **Never encourage a customer to “push through” breathing difficulty, anxiety or skin reaction.**

## 5. Evidence boundary and claim policy

### Evidence summary
Current published evidence does **not** support mouth taping as a broadly established clinical treatment for mouth breathing, sleep-disordered breathing or OSA. A 2025 systematic review identified only 10 included studies / 213 patients; some small studies reported improvements in selected markers, while others found no benefit, and the review highlighted potential harm when nasal obstruction is present.

### CLAIM GREEN — allowed subject to final packaging/legal review
Low-risk factual/mechanical claims:
- “A gentle physical cue to help keep the lips together.”
- “Designed for use as part of a bedtime routine.”
- “For suitable adults who can breathe comfortably through the nose.”
- Factual material, dimensions, pack count and adhesive specifications once verified.
- “Easy-removal” / “skin-friendly” only if supplier test/specification supports the wording.

### CLAIM AMBER — requires evidence/claims review before publication
Do not publish automatically:
- Helps reduce waking dry-mouth sensation.
- Encourages nasal breathing during sleep.
- May reduce mouth opening in selected users.
- Comfort / adherence claims.
- Skin-sensitivity claims.

Reason: these may be plausible/user-experience claims but need exact wording, substantiation and material verification.

### CLAIM RED — prohibited without a materially different evidence/regulatory position
- Treats/cures/prevents sleep apnoea.
- Replaces CPAP or oral-appliance therapy.
- Treats snoring.
- Improves oxygen saturation.
- Improves deep sleep / REM / sleep quality as a guaranteed physiological effect.
- Lowers blood pressure.
- Improves jawline/facial structure.
- Prevents cardiovascular disease.
- “Clinically proven” as a general sleep treatment.
- “Safe for everyone.”

Marketing rule: do not turn a biological theory about nasal breathing into a product efficacy claim.

## 6. How to use — draft operating instructions

**Final instructions must be reconciled with the actual adhesive design and supplier IFU before publication.**

1. Before bed, confirm you can breathe comfortably through your nose.
2. Clean and dry the skin around the lips; avoid applying over creams/oils that interfere with adhesion.
3. Apply the product using the pack-specific placement method. Do not improvise a stronger/full occlusion than the product is designed to provide.
4. Check that you remain comfortable and can remove the product easily.
5. Remove on waking, or immediately at any time if breathing feels restricted, anxiety occurs or the skin becomes uncomfortable.
6. Discontinue repeated use if irritation or sleep disruption occurs.

### First-use UX
Website/app should recommend first familiarising the user with application/removal while fully awake. It must not frame discomfort as a sign that the product is “working.”

## 7. What to expect

### Good expectation framing
- It should feel like a light reminder rather than a forceful restraint.
- Some users may decide it is not comfortable or suitable; that is an acceptable outcome.
- Sleep Tape addresses one behaviour only. Sleep timing, stress/arousal, environment, alcohol, nasal health and possible sleep disorders may matter more for an individual user.

### Bad expectation framing
- “You will sleep better tonight.”
- “This stops snoring.”
- “This forces nasal breathing and increases oxygen.”
- “Use it for X nights and your mouth breathing will be cured.”

## 9. FAQ master

### What does Sleep Tape do?
It provides a gentle physical cue to help suitable adults keep their lips together during sleep. It is designed as part of a bedtime routine, not as a treatment for a sleep or breathing disorder.

### Does it stop snoring?
We should not promise that. Snoring has many causes and can sometimes be a sign of OSA. Sleep Tape is not an approved substitute for assessment or treatment of persistent/problematic snoring.

### Can I use it if I have sleep apnoea?
Do not use it as a replacement for CPAP, an oral appliance or other prescribed treatment. If you have diagnosed/suspected OSA, obtain clinical advice about suitability rather than self-treating with mouth tape.

### What if my nose is blocked?
Do not use it while your nose is blocked or nasal breathing is uncomfortable.

### What if I feel anxious or cannot breathe comfortably?
Remove it immediately. Do not try to force yourself to continue.

### Can I use it every night?
Only if it remains comfortable and suitable for you and there is no breathing or skin problem. Repeated irritation, anxiety or sleep disruption is a reason to stop.

### What if I get a rash?
Stop using it and allow the skin to recover. Significant swelling, breathing difficulty or a serious allergic reaction requires appropriate medical care.

### Is it scientifically proven to improve sleep?
Evidence on mouth taping remains limited and mixed. We therefore position Sleep Tape as a behavioural sleep-routine accessory rather than claiming it treats sleep disorders or guarantees better sleep.

## 11. Sleep Intelligence bridge

Sleep Tape must never become the default answer merely because the user reports poor sleep.

### Candidate signals before recommendation
- User reports habitual mouth-open sleep / mouth-opening concern.
- User reports ability to breathe comfortably through nose.
- No current congestion/obstruction signal.
- No OSA red-flag/suspected disorder signal.
- No relevant product intolerance signal.

### Corroboration rule
A single “I snore” answer is **not** sufficient to recommend Sleep Tape. Snoring should trigger symptom clarification / safety routing first.

### Recommendation confidence
- HIGH enough to present as optional routine aid: mouth-opening concern + comfortable nasal breathing + no red flags.
- LOW / do not recommend: congestion, nasal obstruction, OSA red flags, respiratory distress, unclear ability to nasal-breathe, or user seeks treatment claim.

### Outcome tracking
Track subjective, non-diagnostic outcomes only unless validated clinical instrumentation is introduced:
- used / not used;
- comfortable / uncomfortable;
- stayed on / removed;
- perceived morning mouth dryness change;
- perceived sleep disruption;
- skin irritation;
- reason discontinued.

Do not infer OSA improvement from consumer self-report.

## 12. Growth / marketing content atom

### Research/product fact
Keeping the mouth closed is sometimes promoted online as a way to improve sleep, but evidence for mouth taping as a clinical sleep treatment is limited and safety depends on the person being able to breathe comfortably through the nose.

### Customer problem
“I wake with my mouth open and want a simple way to remind myself to keep my lips together.”

### Safe message
**Use a cue, not a cure.**

### 20–30 sec short — master concept
Hook: “Mouth tape shouldn’t be sold as a miracle sleep cure.”  
Body: “For the right person, it can simply act as a gentle cue to keep the lips together. But if your nose is blocked, breathing is uncomfortable, or you suspect sleep apnoea, tape is not the answer.”  
Bridge: “Asclepios connects the physical tool to the bigger question: what is actually disturbing your sleep?”  
CTA: “Start with suitability, then build the routine.”

### Compliance guardrail
Marketing may educate about the trend/evidence gap, but must not create fear to drive purchase or imply that Asclepios Sleep Tape uniquely solves OSA/snoring.
